Exercice 1 - manual warm up 1

Imagine having 3 dices (one is blue, the other green and the last one is red) with 3 faces each, and values going from 1 to 3. Consider the sum of the three dices as random variable \(X_{bgr}\):

  1. Compute the probability of obtaining 5, with the blue dice being 2, the red being 2, and the green being 1: \(P(X_{bgr} = 5_{212})\)
  2. What is the set of potential outcomes, \(S_X\) of \(X_{bgr}\) ?
  3. Now imagine that the dices can not be distinguished anymore, your random variable is \(X\). What is your probability of obtaining 3, \(P(X=3)\) ?
  4. What is the probability of obtaining 5 ?
  5. How much is the expectation ?
  6. You know that one of your dices is worth 3. What is the probability of obtaining 5 in total?
  7. Draw a bar plot, showing the potential outcomes in the x axis, and the probabilities in the y axis.
  8. What is the conditional expectation worth in the case where one of the three dices is worth 3 ? Is that equal to the non-conditional expectation of X ?
  9. Verify all your results by simulating the rolling of these three dices, 10000 times, in R.

Exercice 2 - Playing with RStudio

Open RStudio on your personal computer (or your personal coding environment).

  1. Create a new project named “Session 1 - Methods R&PE”, in a new directory. What are the different files created ? Quit the session, go to your folder on your personal computer, and click on the ‘.Rproj’ file created. What happens ?
  2. Write 1+1 in your main .R script and press “Run”.
  3. Create a new folder in this project named ‘data’.
  4. Install packages “tidyverse” and “rio” in the console.
  5. You’re all set for importing data and start creating functions/the next exercices !

Exercice 3 - playing with data

The European social survey “measures the attitudes, beliefs and behaviour patterns of diverse populations in more than thirty nations”. Use the rio package to import data from <“https://jan-rovny.squarespace.com/s/ESS_FR.dta”>.

  1. Use glimpse function to have a look at all. Restrict your data set to euftf (views on European integration), gndr (gender), happy, yrbrn(birthyear) and eduyrs (years of education), using function select() from tidyverse.
  2. How many observations are there ? Are the variables categorical or continuous ?
  3. Summarise your data set and comment on the findings, using table() and summary(). Drop the NA values.
  4. Create a new variable age, with the age of the respondents, using mutate(). Use 2026 as reference year.
  5. Plot the distribution of the variables euftf and happy using barplot(table()). What can you say about these distributions? What is the most common answer ? What is the percentage of respondents who have given this answer ?
  6. Plot a histogram of the age of the respondents, with 10 breaks. What is the decade with the most respondents ?
  7. Plot the distribution of happy for gndr == 1 and gndr == 2, using ggplot2 : ggplot() and geom_bar(position = "dodge")
  8. Does being male/female influence your chances of self-reporting being happy ? Conduct a t-test to test the hypothesis that gender influences people’s self assessed level of happiness. What are the 95% and 99% confidence intervals ?

Exercice 4 : the median income in French rural communes

You want to analyse the electoral outcomes of the radical right in rural communes in France. For this, you have data on the 2024 European elections at https://github.com/paulservais-1/methods_policyevaluation/raw/main/slides/session1/data/elections2024.csv, and data on the French municipalities at https://github.com/paulservais-1/methods_policyevaluation/raw/main/slides/session1/data/demographics2024.xlsx.

  1. Use rio to import both data sets, stored in two different variables
  2. Use glimpse to . Look at the types of codedep and codecommune. Can you merge on codedep and codecommune in these conditions ? Use as.numeric() for both to prepare for the merge
  3. Use the left_join() function to merge both data set on codecommune and codedep.
  4. Restrict your data frame to rural communes, using filter.
  5. Replace NA values by the mean of the variable.
  6. What is the range of the far-right vote ? in absolute and relative terms ?
  7. What is the average of the relative far-right vote in French rural communes ? The median ? The standard deviation ?
  8. Plot the distributions of far-right voting in the different kinds of French rural communes (present in dens7), using ggplot().
  9. Is the expected far-right voting significantly different in those different rural typologies ? Compute different t-tests, and conclude.
